# EmissioNex Demonstration Energy Investigation Guide
Document ID: DEMO-ENERGY-GUIDE-001
Document type: Demonstration reference guide
Status: Synthetic project guidance — NOT a manufacturer manual or regulatory standard

This guide provides general, low-risk investigation steps for a prototype recommendation system.
Technicians must follow approved site procedures, manufacturer instructions, and safety requirements.
A detected energy deviation or SHAP contribution is an investigation lead, not proof of a physical fault.

## Unexpectedly high energy relative to expected energy
When actual energy is materially above a model's expected value, first verify meter data, timestamp alignment,
units, production volume, machine status, and whether the comparison is against a comparable operating period.
Review recent maintenance logs and alarms. Compare the reading with the equipment's documented normal operating
range before recommending repairs. Do not assume a component failure from energy data alone.

## Energy increase while production is unchanged or lower
Compare energy per unit produced over comparable shifts. Check for changed product mix, cycle time, idle periods,
setup/cleaning cycles, and incomplete production records. If the pattern persists, arrange an inspection by qualified
maintenance personnel using the machine's approved maintenance procedure.

## Unexpected consumption during an off-shift or idle period
Confirm the facility's shift schedule and whether standby, safety, ventilation, or temperature-maintenance loads
are expected to remain on. Verify production records and meter association before treating the reading as avoidable.
Use approved shutdown procedures; do not disable safety-critical systems.

## HVAC equipment energy investigation
Compare the reading with the HVAC schedule, occupancy/operating hours, setpoints, and outdoor/indoor conditions if
those measurements are available. Check filter and coil maintenance records, alarms, and control schedules against
the manufacturer's instructions. Do not diagnose refrigerant loss or a mechanical failure from energy data alone.

## Motor-driven equipment energy investigation
Compare load, production rate, operating hours, and motor/drive alarms. Review maintenance records and follow the
manufacturer's inspection procedure for the motor and drive. Do not advise opening energized equipment or changing
electrical settings without qualified personnel.

## Evidence and uncertainty
Record the incident ID, timestamp, machine/location, actual and expected energy, deviation, and retrieved source.
State which observations support each recommendation. If the retrieved material does not substantiate a specific
repair, recommend data verification or qualified inspection rather than inventing a repair.

## Escalation and safety
Use site-approved lockout/tagout and electrical safety procedures where applicable. Any inspection inside electrical
enclosures or work on energized equipment must be handled by authorized qualified personnel. Recommendations in this
demonstration system are advisory and require human review.
